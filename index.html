How the bench works

Every commission shows a swatch to match. Tap pigments in your stock to add them to the mix — tap again to add more parts, a fourth tap clears it. The color you get is computed the way real paint mixes: pigments absorb light, they don't blend like colored bulbs. Blue and yellow paint truly make green. Red and cyan truly make a dark neutral, not a grey average.

Grind matters. Azurite and malachite shift color with how fine they're ground — a real fact of both minerals. A slider appears once you've picked one.

Some pairs must never touch. Orpiment corrupts verdigris and blackens lead white — real chemistry, not a made-up rule. The bench will warn you and refuse to let you test the daub.

Glaze commissions add a thin translucent layer over your base mix — and the ORDER you lay glazes down changes the result, the same way it does on a real panel. Check your daub under both daylight and candlelight before you commit; two mixes can look identical in one light and different in the other. That is not a bug. It is called metamerism, and it caught real painters too.
